      How it works

      1. Your browser asks SealGuard's relay for your token accounts, Token-2022 mints, stake and nonce accounts and token names. The relay only forwards read calls to a Solana RPC provider and keeps its API key out of the page.
      2. The rules run in your browser: approvals, permanent delegates, transfer hooks, frozen accounts, empty accounts, phishing names, stake withdraw authority, durable nonces, and the wallet account's owner.
      3. Fixes are ordinary Token Program instructions (Revoke, Burn, CloseAccount) and System Program nonce withdrawals, built in your browser, simulated, then signed by your wallet. The code is public, so anyone can read exactly what is sent.
      4. Scanning, revoking approvals, burning phishing tokens and withdrawing nonce accounts are free. Closing empty token accounts carries a 5% fee on the rent they return (none for wallets with the guard): it's one extra transfer in the same transaction, shown in your wallet before you sign.

      What this can't see

      • Most Solana thefts are one signature on a transfer, not a standing approval. SealGuard can't stop you signing on another site. Read your wallet's simulation before every signature, and keep large amounts on a hardware wallet.
      • It reads the chain, not your computer: malware on your device is outside what it can check.
      • NFT phishing airdrops are flagged but not burned here yet; burn them from your wallet app.

      Keep the big amounts somewhere a website can't reach

      A hardware wallet keeps your key on a small device that shows you what you're about to sign and needs a physical press to do it. A phishing site can still ask; it can't sign for you. If this wallet holds more than you'd be comfortable losing, move that part to a hardware wallet and keep this one for daily use.

      • Ledger (Nano S Plus, Nano X, Flex): Solana works through Ledger Live and in Phantom, Solflare and Backpack.
      • Trezor (Safe 3, Safe 5): Solana works in Trezor Suite and in the wallets that support it; check their current coin list before buying.

      Buy only from the maker's own store, never second-hand or from a marketplace seller: a tampered device is the one attack a hardware wallet can't protect you from. If you buy through these links, SealGuard earns a small commission from the maker; the price you pay is the same.
