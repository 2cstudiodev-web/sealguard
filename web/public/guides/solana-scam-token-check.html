Is this Solana token a scam? Seven signs to check

Most tokens that turn up in a wallet uninvited are bait. These are the signs worth checking before you touch one.

Seven signs

  1. You didn't buy it. Tokens that just appear are almost always airdropped advertising for a scam.
  2. Its name is a web address or a "claim" message, like "claim-rewards.xyz Airdrop". The site asks for a signature that empties the wallet.
  3. It has no market price. If no exchange or price feed lists it, it's worth nothing, whatever the name says.
  4. Its issuer can take it back. A Token-2022 permanent delegate can move or burn the token from your wallet without your signature. Regulated tokens use this on purpose; an unknown one is a red flag.
  5. It can't be sold. Non-transferable tokens, or tokens with a transfer hook, can trap you: you can receive them and never move them.
  6. Your account is frozen. The issuer froze it; you can't move the token. Scam tokens are often frozen by their own issuer.
  7. Someone you don't know has an approval on it. An approval lets another address move the token without asking you.

What to do with one

Don't visit any link it shows. If it has no price, you can burn it to remove it from view (SealGuard does this for free when the token has no market price). If it has a price, sell or ignore it instead.

Questions

Can a scam token steal from me just by being in my wallet?

Not by itself. The danger is the site it points to and what you sign there.
